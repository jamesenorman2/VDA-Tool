Assumptions and limitations

  • Estimates of maximum achievable speed on a flat, level, dry road; no gradient, camber, surface or tyre variation, and no driver error.
  • Acceleration uses empirical power-law fits (speed = a·distanceb) per vehicle class, capped at a terminal speed beyond 440 m. They are fitted curves, not first-principles models, and are only as good as their source data.
  • Cornering is steady-state: v = √(μ·r·g), capped at a per-class slip speed. Braking into a curve is assumed to be instantaneous, and a vehicle is assumed able to hold the limit speed throughout the curve.
  • The published fits are very slightly discontinuous where bands join (≤ 0.2 km/h) and speeds are rounded to whole numbers in the table.
  • Start conditions: a standing start begins from rest; an entry speed begins at that speed and accelerates on from it along the same curve; a rolling start begins at the class's top speed (its speed after a 440 m run-up), which is never exceeded.
  • Results support, but do not replace, a competent specialist assessment.
